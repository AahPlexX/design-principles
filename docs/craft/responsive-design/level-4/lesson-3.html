Level 4, Lesson 3: picture and art direction — swapping crops per breakpoint

Not every responsive image problem is about file size. Sometimes the same photo just doesn't work at every shape — a wide establishing shot that reads fine on a desktop banner turns into a wall of background noise once it's squeezed into a narrow mobile strip. That's a different job than anything srcset alone can do.

<picture>
  <source media="(max-width: 600px)" srcset="team-portrait-crop.jpg">
  <img src="team-wide-crop.jpg" alt="The team at their desks">
</picture>
What problem does this markup solve that srcset/sizes alone cannot?

This is called art direction. <source> elements inside <picture> let the browser choose between genuinely different image files based on a condition like viewport width — not just different resolutions of the same crop, but a different framing entirely. On a narrow mobile strip, a wide establishing shot can shrink the subject down to a speck; swapping in a tighter, more vertical crop keeps the actual subject legible instead of just making the same composition smaller.

Full explanation: Responsive Design.
