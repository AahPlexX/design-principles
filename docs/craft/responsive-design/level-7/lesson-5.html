Level 7, Lesson 5: Print and other non-screen media as part of “every screen”

"Every screen" stretches all the way to no screen at all. A page a reader prints out is still an output your layout is responsible for — navigation bars, sidebars, and buttons that only make sense on a live, clickable page are dead weight on paper, and a dark background that looked great on a monitor just burns through ink.

A blog post's on-screen design has a dark navy header, a sticky sidebar of related articles, and a floating "Share" button bar. A reader prints the article to read later. What's the responsive-design fix for that printed page?

Without any print-specific styling, a browser prints the page exactly as it appears on-screen, elements meant for on-screen interaction and all. Hiding what's meaningless on paper and dropping to a plain, ink-friendly background reshapes the page for that output the same way a breakpoint reshapes a layout for a narrower viewport.

Full explanation: Responsive Design.
