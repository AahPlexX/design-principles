Level 4, Lesson 3: Destructive actions shouldn't out-rank the safe path

A project management tool's "delete project" confirmation shows two buttons: "Delete project" rendered as a bold, saturated red filled button, and "Cancel" rendered as a small gray text link beside it. The dialog exists specifically to give someone a chance to back out of something irreversible — but its own styling is pulling the eye toward the option that can't be undone.

In that dialog — "Delete project" as a bold filled red button, "Cancel" as a small gray text link — what's the actual hierarchy problem?

Color-coding a destructive action red is fine on its own — it helps someone recognize what the button does. The mistake is separate: giving that red button the loudest, most dominant styling on the screen while the safer "Cancel" path fades into the background. A confirmation dialog's whole job is to make backing out easy and make the irreversible action require deliberate effort, so the safe option should be at least as visually prominent as the destructive one — often the filled, primary-weight button should be Cancel, with the destructive action styled a level down even though it keeps its warning color. Removing the cancel option or just renaming a label doesn't touch the actual weight imbalance.
