Level 4, Lesson 4: Disabled states carry deliberately reduced weight

A checkout form's "Place order" button won't do anything yet because a required field is still empty — but it's rendered in the same full-strength brand color and contrast as when it's ready to submit. A shopper has no visual reason to suspect the button won't work until after they've clicked it and nothing happened.

What's the correct fix for that "Place order" button while a required field is still empty?

A disabled control is telling the reader "not yet," and hierarchy needs to say that visually before anyone clicks it, not after. Muting the contrast or fill is what signals unavailability at a glance — it's also why accessibility guidelines don't hold a disabled control to the usual contrast requirements that apply to active text and buttons: the reduced contrast is the intended signal, not a failure to meet one. Hiding the button removes the reader's roadmap entirely, since they can no longer see what's coming once the form is complete, and boosting its contrast does the opposite of what a disabled state needs — it makes an inactive action look like the screen's most important one.
