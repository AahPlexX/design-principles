Level 5, Lesson 3: Marketing pages — hero hierarchy, single-message rule

A "hero" is the large section at the top of a landing page — usually a headline, a short line of support copy, and a button — and it's the one part of a page where hierarchy mistakes are the most expensive, because it's the first thing every visitor sees. The same rule that governs a single screen applies here at full force: one headline, one primary action, one message.

A landing page hero has a large headline, "Ship your app faster." Directly below it, in the same large bold size, sits a second line: "Also check out our new integrations marketplace!" Next to each other underneath are two identically styled filled buttons: "Start free trial" and "Browse integrations." What's the hierarchy problem?

A hero only gets a few seconds of a first-time visitor's attention, and it can't spend that on two competing pitches at full volume. The single-message rule means one headline and one primary button carry the hero's full visual weight; a second feature like an integrations marketplace still deserves a place on the page — just further down, in its own section, styled with less weight than the hero's main call to action.
