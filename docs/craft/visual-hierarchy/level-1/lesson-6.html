Level 1, Lesson 6: Position as a signal

Where something sits on a screen changes how important it feels, before size or color get a say. Readers tend to land near the top of a screen first and work outward from there, so whatever occupies that early real estate gets noticed first — and whatever sits at the bottom of a long page, or tucked in a far corner, starts out at a disadvantage no matter how it's styled.

A web app shows a warning — "Your session will expire in 2 minutes" — in a thin strip at the very bottom of a long, scrolling settings page. A promotional banner for the company's newsletter is pinned at the very top, where every visitor lands first. What's the positional hierarchy problem?

A reader's eye lands on predictable spots first — the top of a screen, the center of a layout — before it works outward to the rest of the page. Whatever occupies that first-seen space gets a head start on being noticed, independent of how big or bright it is. A time-sensitive warning belongs where it will actually be seen right away, not below content that matters far less.
