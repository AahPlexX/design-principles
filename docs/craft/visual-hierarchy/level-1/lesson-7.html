Level 1, Lesson 7: Depth and elevation as a signal

A drop shadow, or a dimmed backdrop behind a popped-up panel, simulates something floating above the rest of a flat page — on its own layer, closer to the reader. That simulated distance is what tells the eye "this is on top of everything else, deal with this first," the same way a piece of paper physically lifted off a desk draws your attention before the papers still lying flat.

A confirmation dialog asking "Delete this project?" pops open on top of the page. It uses the exact same flat white background as the page behind it — no shadow, no dimmed backdrop, no border separating the two. What's the hierarchy problem?

A shadow, or dimming everything behind an element, simulates that element floating above the rest of the page on its own layer — that separation is what signals "this is on top, handle this first." Remove the separation and the dialog competes for attention on equal footing with the very content it's supposed to interrupt, so it's easy to miss or click past without noticing.
