Level 1, Lesson 8: Decorative elements must never outweigh the content they support

Size is one of the strongest hierarchy signals there is — which is exactly why it backfires when a purely decorative element is sized larger than the actual message the page needs someone to read. The eye goes to the biggest thing first, decoration or not.

A landing page has a full-width illustration at the top, and below it a headline in small, thin, light-gray text. What's the hierarchy problem?

A large illustration dominates the screen purely by size, while the headline — small and low-contrast — reads as an afterthought. If the headline is what the page actually needs read, it should carry more visual weight than the decoration next to it, not less.
