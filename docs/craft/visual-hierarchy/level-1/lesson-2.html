Level 1, Lesson 2: Size as a hierarchy signal

Before a reader parses a single word, size has already told them where to look. A bigger element reads as more important than a smaller one, automatically — no button labeled "Set as most important" required.

A pricing page has three plan cards: Basic, Pro, and Enterprise. Pro is the plan the business wants most people to choose. All three plan names render at the exact same font size, with the exact same weight, in the exact same color. What's the hierarchy problem?

Size is the fastest, most primitive hierarchy signal there is — a reader's eye registers the biggest thing on screen before they've read any of the words on it. If the business wants people to notice the Pro plan first, something about it — the plan name, the price, or a "Most popular" label — needs to be visibly larger than its neighbors, not matched to them for the sake of tidiness.
