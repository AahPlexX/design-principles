Level 1, Lesson 5: Whitespace and isolation as a signal

Empty space around an element separates it from its neighbors, and separation reads as importance — the same way a single word alone on an otherwise blank slide feels louder than that same word buried in the middle of a paragraph. Give something room to breathe and the eye treats it as its own distinct thing worth noticing.

A landing page's primary "Sign up free" button sits crammed directly between two blocks of paragraph text, with almost no margin above or below it. Meanwhile, a row of minor footer links further down the page each has generous empty space around them. What's the hierarchy problem?

Isolating whitespace is a hierarchy signal on its own — it's what tells the eye "this is worth pausing on," separate from the size or color of the thing itself. The primary button needs more breathing room around it than lower-priority elements, not less; cramming it between paragraphs erases the very separation that would make it pop.
