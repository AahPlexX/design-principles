Level 6, Lesson 5: Zoom and reflow — hierarchy must survive high zoom

Plenty of readers never touch a page at 100% zoom. Some magnify their browser significantly — enough that the page has to reflow into a single narrow column just to keep every line of text on screen without scrolling sideways. A hierarchy that only holds up at one exact zoom level and one exact viewport width isn't really a hierarchy — it's a coincidence that breaks the moment either one changes.

At normal zoom, a landing page has one clear focal point: a large hero headline, with a small "Sign in" link tucked into the corner. A reader zooms their browser in significantly to read the text more comfortably. At that zoom level the layout reflows into a single narrow column, and the "Sign in" link — unchanged in size, but no longer confined to a small corner — now sits visually above the hero headline. What's the hierarchy problem?

Reflow at high zoom often restacks elements that were only sitting side by side, or tucked into a corner, at normal zoom — and restacking can hand the top visual position to something that was only ever meant to be secondary. A hierarchy has to be checked again at significant zoom and at small viewport widths, using the same one-dominant-element rule that applies at 100% — a page hasn't proven its hierarchy holds until it's been tested at both.
