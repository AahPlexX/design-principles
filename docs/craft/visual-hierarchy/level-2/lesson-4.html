Level 2, Lesson 4: Common region

Three plan cards sit on a pricing page. The recommended plan gets a shaded background panel and a border wrapped around it; the other two just sit on the plain page. Nothing about the recommended plan's text or button is actually bigger than the others — the enclosure itself is what makes that whole card read as one elevated unit above the rest.

A dashboard shows four metrics in a row: Revenue, Signups, Churn, and Active Users. Revenue is the number the team actually needs to watch first. Which change makes Revenue read as dominant, without touching any font size or color?

Common region means elements enclosed by a shared boundary — a border, a background tint, a card — are perceived as one distinct group, set apart from whatever sits outside that boundary. Putting a visible container around just the Revenue metric marks it as its own elevated unit compared to the other three, which stay part of the plain dashboard background. No font-size or color change is needed — the enclosure alone does the work of separating it from the rest.
