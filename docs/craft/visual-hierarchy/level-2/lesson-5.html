Level 2, Lesson 5: Continuity and alignment

A hero section's headline, subhead, and button all sit flush against the same left edge, stacked one under another. The eye lands on the headline and glides straight down that edge to the button without having to search for where to look next. Nudge the button a few pixels off that edge, and the eye has to stop and hunt for it instead.

A signup form has every label and input field aligned to the same left edge, except one input that's indented slightly further right than the rest. What's the hierarchy effect, in Gestalt terms?

Continuity is the eye's tendency to keep following a line, edge, or curve it's already tracking — a column of fields aligned to the same edge reads as one continuous path the eye can glide down without stopping. A single field pushed off that edge breaks the line, and the eye has to pause and hunt for where the path continues — which reads as an interruption, not as intentional emphasis. If that field genuinely needs more attention, a deliberate signal like a colored border says so far more clearly than an accidental misalignment does.
