Level 2, Lesson 1: Proximity

A pricing card shows a plan name, a price, and a "Choose plan" button. Push those three close together and they fuse into one loud unit the eye can't miss. Spread them evenly across the card instead, and the same three elements start reading as three separate, weaker things — none of them winning the eye's attention the way the tight cluster would.

A pricing page has three plan cards side by side. One plan is meant to read as the single most dominant thing on the page. Which layout achieves that using proximity alone, without changing any font size or color?

Proximity turns whatever sits close together into one perceived chunk. When the gaps inside a card are clearly smaller than the gap between the card and everything around it, the plan name, price, and button fuse into a single unit that carries more visual force than any one part alone — that's what lets the whole card win the eye's attention first, not just its button. Equal spacing everywhere erases that boundary, and matching the internal gaps to the outer gap makes the card blend into its neighbors instead of standing apart from them.
