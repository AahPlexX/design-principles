Level 2, Lesson 3: Figure/ground

A confirmation dialog asks "Delete this project?" and pops up over the page behind it. That background isn't left alone — it gets dimmed to near-black. The dimming isn't decoration: it's what pushes the rest of the page into the background so the dialog reads as the one thing on screen that matters right now.

A promo banner is supposed to be the most important thing on a page, but its background color is only a shade off from the page color behind it — a pale cream banner sitting on an off-white page. In Gestalt terms, what's the hierarchy problem?

Figure/ground is the perceptual split between the thing that reads as sitting "in front" (the figure) and everything behind it (the ground) — a clear boundary between the two is what lets the figure win the eye's attention first. When a banner's background barely differs from the page behind it, that boundary disappears, and the banner reads as part of the page's ground instead of the dominant figure it's supposed to be. A stronger background color or a visible border would re-establish the separation and hand the banner back the weight it needs.
