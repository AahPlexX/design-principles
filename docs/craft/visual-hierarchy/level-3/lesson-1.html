Level 3, Lesson 1: The F-pattern for text-heavy pages

Give someone a long article and track where their eyes go: a full sweep across the top, a shorter sweep partway down, then a slow drift down the left edge while the right side gets less and less attention. Connect those movements and they trace the shape of the letter F — which is why researchers named this the F-pattern. It shows up on any page with a lot of text and no single dominant element to interrupt it: articles, search results, long product descriptions.

A help-center article has a right-hand sidebar. A third of the way down the page, that sidebar contains a plain-text box reading "Related: upgrade your plan for this feature" — styled with the same size and weight as every other sidebar link. Based on the F-pattern, will most first-time readers notice it?

The F-pattern predicts a top sweep, a second shorter sweep, then a vertical read down the left edge — attention on the right column and further down the page thins out fast. A plain-styled box sitting a third of the way down the right rail sits right in that low-attention zone, so most readers will scroll past it without registering it. Making it noticeable would take a stronger signal — more contrast, size, or color — to pull it out of the default path, not just placement.
