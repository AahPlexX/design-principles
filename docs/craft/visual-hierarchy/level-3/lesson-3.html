Level 3, Lesson 3: The Gutenberg diagram and reading gravity

Not every page is dense with text like an article, or built around one big hero image like a landing page. Sometimes a layout is genuinely uniform — a plain order form, a simple flyer, a row of evenly-sized fields with nothing bolder or brighter than anything else. For pages like that, there's a third pattern, credited to the typographer Edmund Arnold, called the Gutenberg diagram. It splits the screen into four quarters and predicts where a reader's eye starts and where it ends up.

The top-left quarter is the primary optical area — where the eye naturally lands first. The bottom-right quarter is the terminal area — where it naturally ends up. The top-right quarter is the strong fallow area — it gets glanced at, but doesn't hold attention the way the primary optical area does. The bottom-left quarter is the weak fallow area — the least noticed spot on the whole page. The pull from top-left down to bottom-right is called reading gravity: the same left-to-right, top-to-bottom habit built from reading ordinary text. This pattern only holds when the layout is actually uniform — the moment one element is styled to stand out, the diagram stops predicting anything.

A plain contact form has five fields, all the same size, weight, and color, followed by a "Submit" button. Nothing on the form is emphasized more than anything else. Per the Gutenberg diagram, where's the strongest spot for that button, and where's the weakest spot on the page?

The four quadrants are: primary optical area (top-left, where the eye starts), strong fallow area (top-right, glanced at but not dwelt on), weak fallow area (bottom-left, the least-noticed spot of the four), and terminal area (bottom-right, where reading gravity naturally deposits the eye). A "Submit" button belongs in the terminal area because that's where the scan is already heading by the time someone finishes a uniform, low-hierarchy layout like this form — and the weak fallow area, bottom-left, is the worst place to put anything you want noticed.
