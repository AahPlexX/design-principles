Level 3, Lesson 6: Above-the-fold priority

"Above the fold" comes from newspapers — the part of a folded paper visible on a newsstand rack without anyone unfolding it. On a screen, it means whatever fits inside the browser window the moment a page finishes loading, before anyone touches a scrollbar or a trackpad. Content above that line gets seen automatically, with no action required from the reader. Content below it only gets seen if the reader chooses to scroll down looking for it — and that choice is never guaranteed.

A pricing page opens with a full-screen hero photo and a headline that never mentions price. The actual plan comparison table and "Choose plan" buttons sit entirely below that, reachable only by scrolling. A visitor lands on the page and closes the tab without ever scrolling. What's the best explanation, based on above-the-fold priority?

Whatever's above the fold gets seen the instant the page renders — no scrolling, no decision, no effort required. Everything below it depends entirely on the reader choosing to scroll, and a hero and headline that say nothing about pricing gave this visitor no reason to make that choice. The pricing table wasn't broken or slow to load — it simply sat in the part of the page this visitor never reached.
