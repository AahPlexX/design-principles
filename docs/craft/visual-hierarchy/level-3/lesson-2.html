Level 3, Lesson 2: The Z-pattern for sparse layouts

A page with very little text — a single-screen landing page, a logo and one headline and one photo — doesn't give the eye enough words to fall into the F-pattern's line-by-line read. Instead, eye-tracking on pages like this shows a different path: a sweep from top-left to top-right, a diagonal cut down to the bottom-left, then a final sweep across to the bottom-right. Traced on the page, it looks like the letter Z.

A single-screen product page has a logo in the top-left, an account link in the top-right, a one-line pitch with a hero photo in the middle, and one "Start free trial" button still left to place. Which corner puts that button at the natural final stop of the page's Z-pattern scan?

The Z-pattern starts top-left, sweeps to top-right, cuts diagonally to bottom-left, then finishes with a sweep to bottom-right. That last stop is where the eye naturally ends up after tracing the rest of a sparse page, which is exactly why the primary action button on a landing page conventionally lands in the bottom-right (or centered along that bottom sweep) — it's the spot the scan was already heading toward.
