Level 6, Lesson 1: Consistent margin on mobile vs. cramming edge-to-edge

A phone screen is narrower, not more precious. Treating every one of its pixels as too valuable to spend on margin is how a page ends up with text pressed flat against the glass instead of a screen's worth of space to actually read it in.

A settings screen has 32px of margin on the left and right of its content on desktop. To "fit more onto the screen" on a phone, someone sets that margin to 0, so every label, toggle, and paragraph now runs edge-to-edge with the glass. What's the actual result?

Margin around a page's content isn't wasted space — it's what keeps text and controls from reading as cramped and keeps touch targets away from the edge, where a thumb is least accurate. Removing it doesn't make the content easier to use, and it doesn't buy back meaningful room either: a line of text wraps at roughly the same point whether the margin is 32px or 0, because the text itself is still the same size. The margin a layout uses on desktop should shrink on a narrow screen if it needs to, but it shouldn't disappear — keep a consistent, smaller margin instead of none at all.
