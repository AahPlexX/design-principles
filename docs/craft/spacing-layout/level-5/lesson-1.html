Level 5, Lesson 1: Macro whitespace vs. micro whitespace

Picture a settings page with three sections stacked in one column: Account, Notifications, and Billing. There's a 48px gap between the end of one section and the start of the next. Inside the Account section, the label "Display name" sits only 8px above its own input. Both of those are whitespace, but they're solving different problems at different scales.

A settings page uses a 48px gap between its Account, Notifications, and Billing sections, and an 8px gap between each field's label and its own input. What term describes the 48px gap between sections?

Macro whitespace is the large-scale space between major blocks of a layout — the 48px gap here separates one whole section from the next, so a reader can tell where "Account" ends and "Notifications" begins without reading a single word. Micro whitespace is the small-scale space inside one piece of content, like the 8px gap that ties a label to its own input. Both are doing real work, just at different zoom levels: macro whitespace organizes the page, micro whitespace organizes a component.
