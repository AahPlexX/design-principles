Level 3, Lesson 2: When centering is actually correct

A designer just learned "don't center everything" and takes it literally: they left-align the confirmation dialog that pops up after checkout. It's a small floating box with one short headline, one line of body text, and one button — now all hugging the left wall of the box, with a wide, empty gap of nothing on the right.

A confirmation modal is 400px wide and holds only a short headline, one line of body text, and one button, floating in the middle of the screen with nothing else beside it. Should its content be centered or left-aligned?

The "don't center everything" rule is about content a reader scans down — a page of paragraphs, a stack of form fields, a list of cards — where centering removes the one consistent edge the eye needs to track from item to item. A small modal with a single headline, one line of text, and one button has no "next item" to scan toward; it's one isolated block sitting in its own box. Centering it keeps the whole block visually balanced instead of leaving it looking like it's sliding off the left wall of a small floating box. The same logic applies to standalone hero headlines and empty-state messages (like a "No results yet" icon with a short line of text and a button) — short, self-contained content reads fine centered; anything with a next line or a next row to compare against needs a shared edge instead.
