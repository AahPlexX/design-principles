Level 3, Lesson 4: Aligning icons to a text baseline

A settings screen has a small lock icon just before the label "Private account." In the design file, the icon and the text line up perfectly. In the built page, the icon floats a few pixels above the label, like it's trying to escape the row.

A small lock icon sits inline right before the text "Private account," but it renders a few pixels higher than the label even though both are on the same line. What's the most likely cause, and the fix?

The baseline is the invisible line most letters sit on — like the ruled line on lined paper. A browser's default inline alignment lines up the bottom of an icon with that baseline. But most lowercase letters (like the "e" and "a" in "account") don't fill the space from baseline to cap-height (the height of a capital letter) — their visible ink sits in a shorter band in the middle. An icon that's as tall as a capital letter and sits right on the baseline ends up taller and higher than that lowercase band, so it reads as floating above the word instead of sitting level with it. The fix is to stop relying on default inline/baseline alignment and explicitly center the icon against the text's visual middle — most simply by making the icon and label a flex row with align-items: center.
