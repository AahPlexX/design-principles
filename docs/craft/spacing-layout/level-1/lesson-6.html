Level 1, Lesson 6: Closure — a grid reads as a bounded group

A product gallery shows nine thumbnails in a 3-by-3 grid. There's no border, no background tint, and no box drawn around the group — just a consistent 16px gap between every thumbnail and a matching 16px margin between the outermost thumbnails and the rest of the page. Users still describe it, without hesitation, as "the gallery" — one thing, not nine loose images.

Nothing visually marks the outer edge of the 3-by-3 thumbnail grid — no line, no shaded panel. Why do users still perceive the nine thumbnails as one bounded group rather than nine separate images scattered on the page?

Closure is the mind's habit of filling in a shape from incomplete visual information — seeing a boundary where none is drawn, as long as the surrounding cues are consistent enough to imply one. A grid with even gaps between items and a matching margin around the whole set gives the eye exactly that cue: it completes the missing rectangle and reads the group as one bounded unit, no border or background required.
