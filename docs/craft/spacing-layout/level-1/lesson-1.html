Level 1, Lesson 1: Proximity — closer reads as related

You're reviewing a signup form before it ships. The copy reads fine and the colors pass contrast, but something about the layout still makes it hard to tell which label goes with which field. The fix isn't in the wording — it's in the gaps.

A form uses margin-bottom: 24px between one field group and the next, and margin-bottom: 8px between a field's label and its own input. Why does this combination make the grouping easy to read?

Space between related items should always be smaller than the space between unrelated groups. Here, the gap between a label and its own input (8px) is clearly smaller than the gap between one field group and the next (24px) — proximity tells the reader what belongs together before they even read the labels.
