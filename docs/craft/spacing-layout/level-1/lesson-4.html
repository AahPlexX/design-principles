Level 1, Lesson 4: Alignment as a grouping signal

A checkout summary lists three lines — Subtotal, Tax, Total — with their dollar amounts right-aligned to the same vertical edge on the panel's right side. The line spacing between them is nothing special, just a plain 8px. But something about the layout makes the three amounts read as one connected column of numbers, clearly separate from the "Place order" button underneath.

The three dollar amounts (Subtotal, Tax, Total) all line up on the same right edge. If the "Total" amount were shifted 6px to the left of that shared edge — everything else unchanged — what would happen to how the summary reads?

Items that share an edge — a left edge, a right edge, a baseline — read as connected, independent of the gap between them. That's why the three amounts in the checkout summary feel like one list: the shared right edge gives the eye an invisible line to follow down the column. Breaking that shared edge, even by a few pixels, breaks the grouping signal, because alignment is a separate cue from spacing — a layout can have consistent gaps and still read as disorganized if nothing actually lines up.
