Level 1, Lesson 5: Figure/ground — how much space reads as foreground

A product card has a thin 1px border, but only 2px of padding between that border and the image and text inside it. Everything the card needs is there — image, title, price — yet reviewers keep describing the card as "cramped" and "hard to scan," even though nothing inside it is actually missing or too small.

Widening the card's internal padding from 2px to 16px, without changing anything else, is the fix reviewers are asking for without knowing the name for it. Why does more space between the card's edge and its content make that content easier to read as "the card," instead of just clutter near a border?

Figure/ground is the perceptual split between the thing you're looking at (the figure) and everything behind or around it (the ground). Padding is what creates that split in a layout: enough empty space around content lets the eye register it as a distinct object sitting in the foreground, rather than something crowding the boundary it's inside. A border alone doesn't fix this — a border with almost no padding just moves the crowding right up against a visible line instead of an invisible one.
