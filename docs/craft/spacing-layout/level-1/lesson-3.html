Level 1, Lesson 3: Common region — a shared background groups items

A settings page has a "Notification preferences" section: three toggles sit inside a card with a light gray background and 16px of padding. The gap between this card and the next section on the page is only a little bigger than the gap between the toggles inside it. Despite that small difference, nobody has trouble telling where the notification group ends and the next section begins.

Inside the gray card, the toggles are spaced 12px apart. Below the card, the next section starts 20px after the card's bottom edge — only 8px more than the spacing inside it. Why is the boundary between the two sections still obvious?

A shared background or border draws a boundary the eye can see directly, so it doesn't have to infer grouping from gap size the way it does with plain proximity. That's why a card with a light background can group its contents clearly even when the gap separating it from the next section isn't dramatically bigger than the gaps inside it — the visible edge is doing the work that space alone would otherwise have to do.
