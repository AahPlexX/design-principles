Level 4, Lesson 5: Logical properties vs. physical ones

A component pushes an icon away from its label with margin-left: 16px — a "physical" property, meaning it always means the literal left side of the screen, no matter what language is on it. The product later ships in Arabic, a right-to-left (RTL) language where text and layout flow from the right instead of the left. That hardcoded left margin now sits on the wrong side of the icon, and nobody wants to hunt down every margin-left in the codebase to mirror it by hand.

A component pushes an icon away from its label with margin-left: 16px. The product now needs to support Arabic (RTL) without maintaining a separate, mirrored stylesheet. What's the fix, and why does it work?

margin-left always means the same physical edge of the screen, regardless of language. margin-inline-start is a logical property: it means "the margin at the edge where a line of text begins," which CSS resolves differently depending on the page's writing mode and direction. In English (left-to-right), margin-inline-start resolves to margin-left; in Arabic or Hebrew (right-to-left), the same declaration resolves to margin-right — with no separate rule, no mirrored stylesheet, and no direction-specific override needed.
