Level 4, Lesson 4: Padding vs. margin

A teammate asks you to "add some space" to a yellow notification card — the text inside touches the card's edges, and the card also sits flush against the card above it. "Add space" isn't specific enough: padding is the space between an element's own edge and its content, still inside that element's boundary (its background color fills padding too); margin is the space between an element's edge and whatever sits outside it, and it never picks up that element's background. Picking the wrong one fixes the wrong gap.

A card has a yellow background. The text inside touches the card's edges, looking cramped, and the card itself also sits flush against the card above it, with no visible separation. Which pair of fixes solves both problems?

Padding lives inside the boundary: it pushes content away from the edges of its own box, and the box's background color extends underneath it, which is exactly what the cramped text needs. Margin lives outside the boundary: it's invisible space between one element and its neighbors, never filled by that element's own background — which is exactly what's needed to separate the two cards. Reaching for the wrong one either does nothing visible (margin on cramped inner text does nothing to the text itself) or produces the wrong kind of gap.
