Level 6, Lesson 3: Video captions need their own outline/scrim

A photo's background is fixed the moment you check it. Video isn't — the frame behind a caption keeps changing for as long as the caption is on screen, so whatever passed a minute ago might not still be true a minute from now.

A product video burns open captions directly onto the footage: plain white text, no background box, no outline. The video cuts between a dim indoor interview and a bright, snow-covered exterior shot later on. The team checks the captions once, during the dim interior segment, confirm they're readable, and ship the whole video as-is. What's the real risk here?

Checking a caption once, against one frame, only proves that frame is fine — it says nothing about every other frame the caption will sit over for the rest of the video. Because the background keeps moving, captions need a treatment that holds up regardless of what's playing underneath: a persistent semi-opaque bar behind the text, or a solid outline/drop shadow around each letter, rather than relying on the plain text color to keep passing scene after scene.
