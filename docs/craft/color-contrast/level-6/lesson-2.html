Level 6, Lesson 2: A gradient can pass at one end and fail at the other

A gradient scrim isn't one background — it's a range of backgrounds, from its darkest end to its lightest. Testing it once, at one point along that range, only ever tells you about that one point.

A banner's scrim is solid black at 80% opacity along the bottom, fading to fully transparent at the top, laid over a bright photo. A three-line caption is vertically centered across that scrim, so its bottom line sits in the dark, opaque part and its top line sits in the nearly transparent part. The developer measures contrast only where the bottom line sits, confirms it passes, and ships all three lines. What's wrong?

A gradient changes continuously, so "the scrim passes" isn't a single true-or-false — it depends on exactly where along the gradient you're standing. The fix is to check contrast at every distinct point text touches (here, the top line and the bottom line separately), or to keep text confined to the part of the gradient that's reliably dark enough, rather than letting it drift into the fading edge.
