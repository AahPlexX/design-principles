Level 6, Lesson 1: Text over photos needs a tested scrim, not a guess

A photograph is never one color. A single hero image can have a bright sky in one corner and a shadowed cliff in another, and a headline sitting across both is really sitting across dozens of different backgrounds at once — not one.

A travel site's hero banner puts a white headline directly over a landscape photo: mostly pale sky and cloud, with a dark strip of mountain along the bottom edge. The designer samples the color behind the headline where it happens to cross the dark mountain, confirms it clears the ratio, and ships it. What's the actual problem?

One sampled pixel only describes that one pixel. The fix isn't sampling harder — it's a scrim: a solid or gradient dark panel placed behind the entire text area so the background the text sits on is consistent and controlled, rather than whatever the photo happens to be doing at that spot. Once the scrim is in place, test the ratio against the lightest part of the image the text overlaps, since that's the part most likely to fail.
