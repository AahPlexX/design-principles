Level 2, Lesson 4: Helper text and captions still owe the full minimum

Helper text under a form field, a timestamp under a comment, a caption under an image — these all get styled smaller and grayer than the content around them, because they're secondary. That's a reasonable visual choice. But "secondary" describes how loud the text looks, not whether the contrast minimum applies to it. If it's normal-sized text, it still needs 4.5:1; if it happens to qualify as large text, it still needs 3:1. There's no "helper text" exemption in the rule at all.

This one is easy to miss because de-emphasis and illegibility look similar in a mockup — both are "the faint gray text." The difference only shows up when you actually measure it, and it matters most exactly where designers reach for the faintest gray: instructions, warnings, and requirements that are genuinely necessary to read, just not the loudest thing on the screen.

A signup form shows "Password must contain at least 8 characters" in light gray below the password field, measuring 2.8:1 against the white background. Does this pass?

Helper text like a password requirement is information the reader actually needs, at normal text size — it gets no exemption. 2.8:1 fails the same 4.5:1 minimum any other body text would have to clear. Being visually secondary is a styling decision; it isn't a reason to drop below the readability floor.
