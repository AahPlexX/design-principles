Level 4, Lesson 4: Choosing a status palette that survives colorblind simulation

Once you know contrast ratio won't catch hue confusion, the fix has to happen at the palette level, before anything gets measured. A status palette — success, warning, error, info, and so on — survives colorblind simulation when each color differs in lightness as well as hue, not hue alone. Lightness is the one signal that stays visible across every pattern of color vision deficiency, including full monochromacy. Picking colors that only differ in hue, tuned to matching lightness, is the exact setup that collapses once you simulate it.

A designer picks four status colors — green, yellow, red, and blue — and deliberately tunes them to the same lightness so the palette "feels balanced" at a glance. Why does this choice work against colorblind users specifically?

Lightness differences hold up under every pattern of color vision deficiency, including full monochromacy, because telling light from dark isn't a hue judgment. Hue differences don't hold up the same way — that's exactly what red-green and blue-yellow deficiencies scramble. A palette tuned to equal lightness throws away the reliable signal and leaves the unreliable one to do all the work.
