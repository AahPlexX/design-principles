Level 3, Lesson 3: Focus indicators need double contrast

A focus indicator — the ring or outline that appears around whatever a keyboard user has tabbed to — sits between two things at once: the control it's wrapped around, and the page behind that control. Checking it against only one of those two surfaces tells you half the story, and the half you skipped is exactly where it tends to fail.

A site's focus ring is a solid light-blue outline. Checked against the white page background, it's easy to see. But several buttons on that page are themselves light blue — and against those buttons, the same ring nearly disappears. A developer says: "we already tested the focus ring against the page, it passes, ship it." What's missing from that test?

A focus indicator has two adjacent surfaces, not one: the page it sits on, and the control it outlines. Testing against only the page misses the case where the indicator and the control share a similar color — exactly what happened with a light-blue ring on a light-blue button. Removing the ring instead of fixing its contrast would leave keyboard users with no visible focus at all, which is a worse failure than the one being fixed.
