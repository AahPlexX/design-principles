Level 9, Lesson 2: Not a safe target yet

A more perceptually accurate scoring method isn't automatically a safer one to build against. APCA's own math has kept changing as it's studied further, and the accessibility guidelines group examining it pulled that contrast work back out for more review instead of locking it in as the official replacement. Until a method is finalized and stable, it's a research direction to keep an eye on — not a number you can point to later and say "this is what we were required to hit."

A design system's lead engineer wants to delete the existing 4.5:1 / 3:1 contrast check from the team's linter and replace it entirely with an APCA-only threshold, arguing that APCA is the more accurate way to measure readability. Should the team make this swap right now?

Being newer or more sophisticated isn't the same as being settled. APCA's scoring has continued to change while it's studied, and it hasn't been adopted as an official replacement for the current ratio. Swapping it in as your only compliance gate means your "passing" bar could shift under you later with no warning. The 4.5:1 / 3:1 rule is the one you can currently point to as a fixed, agreed-on requirement — treat APCA as something worth watching and experimenting with alongside it, not instead of it.
