Level 5, Lesson 4: Elevation/surface colors need their own contrast pass

Dark interfaces often show depth by making a raised surface — a card, a dropdown, a modal — a lighter shade of gray than the page behind it, since drop shadows barely read against a dark background. That means a single dark theme can have several different background shades stacked on top of each other, not just one.

A dark theme's design tokens define a page background of #121212 and a modal surface of #2c2c2c (lighter, so it reads as "raised" above the page). The theme's body text color was measured and approved against the #121212 page background only. That same text color is then used inside the modal. What should the team check before shipping the modal?

Elevation in dark mode is usually communicated with a lighter surface color rather than a shadow, so a theme can end up with several distinct background shades layered on top of the base. Light-colored text sitting on a lighter, more-elevated surface is closer in lightness to that surface than it was to the darker base, which shrinks the ratio. Each surface shade needs its own contrast check against the text placed on it.
