Level 5, Lesson 3: Saturated colors that work in light mode often glare in dark mode

A brand's link color might sit calmly on a white background, doing its job without calling attention to itself. Move that exact same saturated hue onto a near-black background, and it can start to visually buzz at its edges — uncomfortable to stare at even though the ratio checks out fine on paper.

A design system reuses one saturated blue hex value as the link color in both its light and dark themes, since that same value passes the contrast minimum against both backgrounds. Testers report that in dark mode the blue text feels like it's "vibrating" or glowing and is tiring to read for long stretches, even though nobody complains about it in light mode. What's the right fix?

Contrast ratio measures the luminance gap between two colors — it says nothing about how comfortable a saturated hue feels to look at against a very dark surround. Highly saturated colors can appear to glow or vibrate against near-black backgrounds even when the ratio passes. That's why dark themes typically get their own desaturated or lightened variant of a brand color instead of reusing the light theme's exact value.
