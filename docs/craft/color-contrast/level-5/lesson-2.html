Level 5, Lesson 2: Pure white on pure black causes halation for some readers

Maximum contrast sounds like the safe default: pure white text on pure black background clears every ratio with room to spare. But the sharp edge between the two extremes creates a side effect that a ratio number alone won't show you.

A dark-mode redesign changes body text from an off-white (#e8e8e8) on dark gray (#121212) to pure white (#ffffff) on pure black (#000000), on the theory that maximum contrast is always the safest choice. A reader with astigmatism reports that the text now seems to "glow" or blur at the edges, making it harder to read than the previous version. What's going on?

This is halation: the extreme edge between pure white and pure black can produce a glow or blur for some readers, most often those with astigmatism. It's a real cost of maxing out contrast that the ratio number doesn't capture. Slightly off-white text on a dark gray (not pure black) background is usually more comfortable to read while still clearing the minimum ratio.
