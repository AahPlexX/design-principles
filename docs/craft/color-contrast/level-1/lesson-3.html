Level 1, Lesson 3: The scale: 1:1 to 21:1

Every contrast ratio you'll ever see falls somewhere on one fixed scale. The bottom of that scale means "no usable difference at all," and the top means "as different as two colors can possibly be." Knowing what the two ends actually represent makes every number in between easier to judge at a glance.

A designer builds a "ghost" button meant to stay nearly invisible until hover: a background of #ffffff and label text of #fffffe — one unit different in the blue channel. A contrast checker reports the ratio as 1.0:1. What does that number actually tell you about this button?

The scale runs from 1:1 at the bottom to 21:1 at the top. 1:1 means the lighter and darker color are, for contrast purposes, indistinguishable — which is exactly what's happening here: a one-unit difference in a single channel doesn't move the luminance enough to register. 21:1, the other end of the scale, is the ratio between pure black and pure white — the maximum contrast any two colors can produce. Nothing scores higher than that, and nothing scores lower than 1:1.
