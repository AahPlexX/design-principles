Level 1, Lesson 2: Always lighter over darker

A contrast ratio isn't calculated as "text color divided by background color." It's calculated as "whichever color is lighter, divided by whichever color is darker" — full stop. That one detail decides what happens the moment a design flips which color is doing the foreground job.

A product ships dark navy text (#1a1a2e) on a white card for light mode. For dark mode, the team reuses the exact same two colors but flips their roles: white text on a dark navy card. Does the contrast ratio change between the two versions?

Contrast ratio doesn't care which color is labeled "text" or "background" — it always takes the lighter of the two colors' luminance and divides it by the darker one's. Navy and white produce one fixed ratio no matter which of them is doing the foreground job, so light mode and dark mode built from the same two colors pass or fail identically. What actually changes the ratio is swapping in a different color, not swapping which role a color plays.
