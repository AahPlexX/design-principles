Level 8, Lesson 1: Ghost buttons — outline and label both drift too light together

A ghost button — outlined, no fill — only has two things carrying its contrast: a thin border and a label. Design teams often lighten both at once for a quieter, "secondary" look, and because they moved together, nobody separately checks whether the label on its own still clears the reading minimum a border was never held to in the first place.

A "Cancel" ghost button sits on a white settings card: a 1px border in #d0d5dd and a label in #a0a6b0. Checking the label alone against the white card gives roughly 2.5:1. A teammate argues that since the border and label were picked together and the border looks fine, the label must be fine too. What's wrong with that reasoning?

A button's outline is a shape, held to the lower contrast bar used for interactive graphics; its label is text, held to the higher bar used for anything meant to be read. Lightening both together to get a matched, quiet look hides the fact that they have different pass thresholds — the label needs its own check against the card behind it, independent of how the border looks.
